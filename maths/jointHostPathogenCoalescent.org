* Epidemic pathogen model betwen host generation
We describe the horizontal epidemiological process $\mathcal{J}_t$ pathogen during host generations $n+1$ as an SI infection model with contact rate $\beta$, occuring for time $G$ as

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J}_t: \quad J_0  \rightarrow J_G \quad \text{at rate } \beta(N-J_t)J_t/N.
\end{equation}

with intital conditions $\mathcal{J}_0 = I_n+1$ as the number of infected host offspring. The fraction of infected hosts $i(t) = J_t/N$ follows the the ODE

\begin{equation}
\frac{di}{dt} = \beta i(1-i), \quad \text{with } i(0) = J_0/N
\end{equation}

that has the solution

\begin{equation}
i(t) = \frac{i_0}{e^{-\beta t}(1-i_0) + i_0}.
\end{equation}

For one intial infection this system looks like the following: with the number of infected growing faster in time depending on the transmission rate $\beta$.


#+begin_src python :session :results output :exports none
N = 1000 # Host population size
betas = [0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1] # contact rate
G = 50
t = np.linspace(0, G, 100) # time from 0 -> G
i_0 = 0.4

def SI_process(t, N, beta, i_0):
    i_t = i_0 / (np.exp(-beta * t)*(1 - i_0) + i_0)
    return(i_t)

* Semi-discrete model with vertical and horizontal transmission 
Assuming the number of infected at the end of the generation $n+1$ to be

\begin{equation}
X_{n+1} = I_{n+1} + K_{n+1}
\end{equation}

where $I_{n+1}$ is the number of infected host offspring and $K_{n+1}$ individuals are newly infected due to the epidemiological process $\mathcal{J}_G$ in generation $n+1$.

If we take $N$ to infinity, we get the deterministic limit of the model, and with $x_n = X_n/N$ we have $I_{n+1}/N \approx c_v x_n$. For the stationary solution $x_n = x_{n+1} = x$ we get

\begin{align}
x = x^* &= \frac{c_v - (1 -c_v)e^{-\beta G}}{c^2_v+(1-c_v)c_v(1-e^{-\beta G})} \\
&= \frac{c_v -e^{-\beta G} + c_v e^{-\beta G}}{c_v + c_v e^{-\beta G} + c_v^2 e^{-\beta G}} 
\end{align}

cVs = [1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1] # probability of vertical transmission

    return(x)

** Ancestral process $A_n$
# started with ChatGPT derivation
Ancestral process $A_n$ denotes the number of pathogen lineages $n$ generations backwards in time, decreating whenever two lineages coalesce. Suppose you start with $k$ pathogen samples at the present generation, the number of lineages starts  $A_0=k$, and goes through the process $k \rightarrow k-1 \rightarrow k-2 \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow2 \rightarrow1$. If $N$ is large that higher order terms disappear $p_c = \mathcal{O}(N^{-1}) << 1$, the ancestral process can be defined as


$$P(A_{n+1}=A_n-1\mid A_n) \approx p_c {A_n\choose2}$$
gives you the *transition probability of the lineage-counting process*.

From that, you can construct the entire ancestral process:
$$ n \xrightarrow{\lambda_n} n-1 \xrightarrow{\lambda_{n-1}} n-2 \rightarrow \cdots \xrightarrow{\lambda_2} 1.$$

where $\lambda_k$ is the coalescence rate when there are $k$ ancestral lineages.
Suppose there are currently $k$ ancestral lineages. There are ${k\choose2}$ possible pairs, and each pair coalesces with probability $p_c$. Therefore, the probability of *a coalescence in the next generation* is

$$\lambda_k = p_c {k\choose 2} = p_c\frac{k(k-1)}{2}$$

with waiting times $T_k$ until the next coalescence approximately geometric distributed

$$P(T_k=t\mid A=k) = (1-\lambda_k)^{t-1}\lambda_k, \qquad t=1,2, \ldots $$

and consequently

$$E[T_k] = \frac{1}{\lambda_k} = \frac{2}{p_c k(k-1)}.$$

Define $T_{n}$ as the time backwards to the most recent common ancestor of $n$ samples, where $T_k$ is the waiting time distribution for the process to go from $k$ lineages to $k-1$. Therefore, it follows for large sample size $n$

\begin{align}
E[T_{n}] &= \sum_{k=2}^n E[T_k] \\
&= \frac{2}{p_c}\sum_{k=2}^n\frac{1}{k(k-1)} = \frac{2}{p_c} \left(1-\frac1n\right) \approx \frac{2}{p_c}.
\end{align}




#+ATTR_LATEX: :options [title=The Wright-Fisher model and Kingman's coalescent]
#+begin_tcolorbox
If, as in a haploid Wright--Fisher model,
$$
p_c=\frac{1}{N},
$$
then
$$
P(A_{n+1}=k-1\mid A_n=k)
\simeq \frac{k(k-1)}{2N}.
$$
and
$$
E[T_k]=\frac{2N}{k(k-1)}.
$$
The time to the most recent common ancestor becomes:
$$E[T_n] = 2N\left(1-\frac1n\right).$$
And for large sample size $n$, this is the familiar Kingman coalescent result.
$$E[T_{n}]\approx 2N.$$
#+end_tcolorbox

**** 3. More generally: the distribution of the MRCA time

You can go beyond its expectation. For large $N$, the geometric waiting times can be approximated by exponential random variables:

$$ T_k\sim \operatorname{Exp}(\lambda_k),$$

with

$$
\lambda_k
=
p_c {k\choose2}.
$$

Thus

$$
T_{\mathrm{MRCA}}
\approx
\sum_{k=2}^n
\operatorname{Exp}
\left(
p_c {k\choose2}
\right).
$$

This is a *hypoexponential distribution*.

For the diploid Wright--Fisher case,

$$
\lambda_k
=
\frac{k(k-1)}{4N},
$$

so

$$
T_{\mathrm{MRCA}}
\approx
\sum_{k=2}^n
\operatorname{Exp}
\left(
\frac{k(k-1)}{4N}
\right).
$$

Consequently,

$$
\frac{T_{\mathrm{MRCA}}}{4N}
\approx
\sum_{k=2}^n
\operatorname{Exp}\left({k\choose2}\right),
$$

where the exponentials on the right have rates ${k\choose2}$.

**** 4. The important distinction for your model

There are actually *two levels* here.

The MRCA time is simply the *hitting time of state 1*:

$$
T_{\mathrm{MRCA}}
=
\inf\{t:A_t=1\}.
$$

Its expectation follows by summing the waiting times between successive states.

**** 5. Important caveat

The approximation

$$
P(A_{t+1}=k-1\mid A_t=k)
\simeq p_c {k\choose2}
$$

assumes that coalescence events are sufficiently rare that *multiple pairwise collisions in the same generation can be neglected*.

For fixed $k$ and $N\to\infty$, this is exactly the regime leading to Kingman's coalescent.

If your pathogen model has substantial offspring skew, however, this approximation can break down: you can have $k\to k-2$, $k\to k-3$, etc. in a single generation.

In that case the MRCA calculation needs the *full transition distribution of $A_n$* rather than just the pairwise coalescence probability.

That distinction is particularly important if your $A_n$ is intended to describe a non-Kingman pathogen genealogy.
