\begin{nofillbox}{Introduction}
 \begin{minipage}{0.79\textwidth}
   \begin{minipage}{0.49\linewidth}
     \textbf{Coevolutionary theory}\\
     Models of reciprocal evolution between hosts and pathogen, including frequency-dependent selection and interaction matrices of host and pathogen genotypes, leading to coevolutionary cycling or arms races.\\
     %\item Extensions include population dynamic feedbacks and recombination, as well as stochastic effects.
     
     While genomic signatures have been summarized with allele frequencies and correlations, there has been \textit{limited genealogical framing of host-pathogen interactions in context of coalescent trees or ancestral recombination graphs (ARGs)}\\

     \textbf{Phylodynamics}\\
     Links pathogen genealogical trees to epidemiological models, and has been used to infer parameters of epidemic transmission from data. But crucially, phylodynamics methods assume host evolution happens on a much slower time scale than pathogen transmission. 
   \end{minipage}
 \hfill
   \begin{minipage}{0.49\linewidth}
     \textbf{Why joint genealogies?}\\
     It is less understood how coevolution shapes the joint relationship of host and pathogen genealogical trees as determined by their repspective infection and reproduction processes.

     More specifically, we aim to understand the how the pathogen ancestral genealogies are embedded within a host genealogy, mediated by horizontal and vertical transmission.\\

     add aim to have a neutral model of how host and pathogen genealogies behave under their joint reproductive processes. (cite Dieter)
     \vfill
   \end{minipage}
 \end{minipage}
 \hfill

  \hfill
  \begin{minipage}{0.56\textwidth}
    % We present a semi-discrete host-pathogen model\\
    
    \textbf{Vertical infection.}
    The host population reproduces in discrete and non-overlapping generations, following a Wright-Fisher process of size \(N\), with $X_{n}$ infected indivduals at the end of generation \(n\). Infected host can produce infected offspring through vertical transmission with probability \(c_v\). At the start of the next generation are \(I_{n+1}\) initally host infected offspring:

    \begin{equation}
    I_{n+1} \sim \text{Binom}(N, c_v X_n/N)
    \end{equation}

    \vspace{20pt}

    \textbf{Horizontal infection.} Continuous-time epidemiological infection process within a host generation running for time $G$ and dynamics following an SI model, with intital conditions \(\mathcal{J}_0 = I_{n+1}\) and the host contact rate $\beta$.
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{J}_t: \quad J_0  \rightarrow J_G \quad \text{at rate } \beta J_t(N-J_t)/N.
    \end{equation}
    
    \vspace{20pt}

    \textbf{Combined process.} The number of infected $X_{n+1}$ at the end of each generation \(n+1\) is the combination of vertical and horizontal infections.
    % The basic reproduction number of the model is $R_0 = c_v e^{\beta G}$, and taking $N \to \infty$ and $x = X/N$, we get the following deterministic solution:
    % \begin{equation}
    %   x^* = \frac{c_v - e^{-\beta G}}{c_v(1-e^{-\beta G})}
    % \end{equation}
    %where \(I_{n+1}\) is the number of infected host offspring and \(K_{n+1}\) individuals are newly infected due to the epidemiological process \(\mathcal{J}_G\) in generation \(n+1\).
  \end{minipage}
\end{nofillbox}

    \hfill
    \begin{minipage}{0.48\textwidth}
        \textbf{Deterministic limit}\\
         Taking $N \to \infty$ and $x = X/N$ we get the following deterministic solution.
        \begin{equation}
        x^* = \frac{c_v - e^{-\beta G}}{c_v(1-e^{-\beta G})}.
        \end{equation}
        \vspace{0pt}

        \textbf{Basic reproductive number: $R_0= c_v e^{\beta G}$}\\
        The persistence threshold for the pathogen ($R_0 >1$) follows for $c_v > e^{-\beta G}$.
        \vspace{35pt}

      \textbf{Ancestral process}\\
      The probability for two randomly sampled pathogen lineages to coalesce within a host generation:
      \begin{equation}
        p_c = \frac{1}{N}+\frac{2(1-c_v)}{c_vx^*N}
      \end{equation}
      % from which we gain the expected coalescence time as $E[T_n] = 2/p_c$.\\
    \end{minipage}
    \end{nofillbox}

      Under complete vertical transmission the genealogy of the pathogen matches that of the host.\\
