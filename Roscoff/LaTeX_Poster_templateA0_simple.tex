\begin{nofillbox}{Introduction}
 \begin{minipage}{0.39\textwidth}
     \textbf{Coevolutionary theory}\\
     Models of reciprocal coevolution between hosts and pathogens, include frequency-dependent selection and interaction matrices of host and pathogen genotypes, leading to coevolutionary cycling or arms races \cite{tellier-2021-theor-host}.\\
     %\item Extensions include population dynamic feedbacks and recombination, as well as stochastic effects.
     While genomic signatures of coevolution have been summarized with allele frequencies and correlations \cite{ebert-2020-host}, there has been \textit{limited genealogical framing of host-pathogen interactions in context of coalescent trees}, limiting our ability to link genomic data to models.\\

     \textbf{Phylodynamics}\\
     Epidemiological dynamics of rapidly evolving pathogens can be generalized as coalescent processes \cite{didelot-2025-ances-proces}. This provides a theoretical framework linking genealogical trees of sampled pathogens to underlying population dynamic processes and thus powerful methods for studying infectious disease outbreaks. Crucially, phylodynamics assumes host evolution is negligible on the timescale of pathogen transmission. 
   \end{minipage}
 \hfill
   \begin{minipage}{0.39\linewidth}
    \textbf{Why joint genealogies?}\\
    It is not well understood how coevolution shapes the joint relationship of host and pathogen genealogical trees as determined by their respective infection and reproduction processes, across both short epidemic and longer coevolutionary time scales.
    More specifically, for infectious diseases the pathogen ancestral lineages are \emph{embedded} within a host genealogy, mediated by horizontal and vertical transmission.\\

    We present a neutral model of joint host--pathogen reproduction and transmission and obtain insights into their joint genealogies using both analytical methods and forward-in time simulations of the coalescent trees using \emph{SLiM} \cite{haller-2023-slim}.

    \textbf{Vertical transmission.}
    The host population reproduces in discrete and non-overlapping generations, following a Wright-Fisher process of size \(N\), with $X_{n}$ infected indivduals at the end of generation \(n\). Infected host can produce infected offspring through vertical transmission with probability \(c_v\). At the start of the next generation are \(I_{n+1}\) initally host infected offspring:

    \begin{equation}
    I_{n+1} \sim \text{Binom}(N, c_v X_n/N)
    \end{equation}

    \vspace{20pt}

    \textbf{Horizontal transmission.} Continuous-time epidemiological infection process within a host generation running for time $G$ and dynamics following an SI model, with intital conditions \(\mathcal{J}_0 = I_{n+1}\) and the host contact rate $\beta$.
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{J}_t: \quad J_0  \rightarrow J_G \quad \text{at rate } \beta J_t(N-J_t)/N.
    \end{equation}
    
    \vspace{20pt}

    \textbf{Combined process.} The number of infected $X_{n+1}$ at the end of each generation \(n+1\) is the combination of vertical and horizontal infections.
    % The basic reproduction number of the model is $R_0 = c_v e^{\beta G}$, and taking $N \to \infty$ and $x = X/N$, we get the following deterministic solution:
    % \begin{equation}
    %   x^* = \frac{c_v - e^{-\beta G}}{c_v(1-e^{-\beta G})}
    % \end{equation}
    %where \(I_{n+1}\) is the number of infected host offspring and \(K_{n+1}\) individuals are newly infected due to the epidemiological process \(\mathcal{J}_G\) in generation \(n+1\).

        \textbf{Deterministic limit.}
         Taking $N \to \infty$ and $x = X/N$ we get the following deterministic solution.
        \begin{equation}
        x^* = \frac{c_v - e^{-\beta G}}{c_v(1-e^{-\beta G})}.
        \end{equation}
        \vspace{0pt}

        \textbf{Basic reproductive number: $R_0= c_v e^{\beta G}$}\\
        The persistence threshold for the pathogen ($R_0 >1$) follows for $c_v > e^{-\beta G}$.
        \vspace{35pt}

      \textbf{Ancestral process.}
      The probability for two randomly sampled pathogen lineages to coalesce within a host generation:
      \begin{equation}
        p_c = \frac{1}{N}+\frac{2(1-c_v)}{c_vx^*N}
      \end{equation}
      % from which we gain the expected coalescence time as $E[T_n] = 2/p_c$.\\

      % \textbf{Key Results}\\
      Under complete vertical transmission ($c_v = 1$) the height of the pathogen coalescent tree $T_n$ matches that of the host.\\

      Low levels of horizontal transmission strongly decouple the joint coalescence times of hosts and pathogens.\\

      High horizontal relative to vertical transmission strongly reduces the pathogen $T_n$.

\begin{tcolorbox}[blankest]
  \begin{nofillbox}{Key Take-Aways}
    We present a semi-discrete model of host and pathogen reproductive processes and their infection process, suitable for discrete generation hosts with epidemic cycles, such as annual plant pathogens. \\

    Our model is a neutral model for studying the joint host and pathogen genealogies for which we derive analytical results and simulations of the embedded coalescence processes under horizontal and vertical transmission.
  \end{nofillbox}
  \vspace{5pt}
